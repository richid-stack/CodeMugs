# 0040. Failover and a circuit breaker in the model gateway

- **Status:** Accepted. Part two of step 8, on top of [ADR 0039](0039-the-model-gateway.md). Budgets, kept usage and the adapter for a real provider are still planned.
- **Date:** 2026-11-25

## Context

[Release gate 16](../../operations/release-gates.md) asks that model failures and provider outages are handled, shown by failover tests with failures injected. A gateway with one provider has nothing to fail over to, and one with several that asks the dead one first, for every call, makes every call wait for a time limit. Two things also have to be decided with care, because they cost money: which failures may be tried again on another provider, and what a failed attempt that was billed is counted as.

## Decisions

1. **Providers are tried in the order given, and only those with room for the request.** The first that is in use and answers is the answer. The attempt is the unit: one call can be several attempts, each against a different provider, **never the same provider twice in one call**. A provider that was just asked and failed is not asked again straight away; that is the breaker's business and the caller's.
2. **Only a failure that is the provider's own moves the call to the next provider**: it could not be reached (`provider_unavailable`), it limited the requests (`rate_limited`) or it was too slow (`timeout`). These are the codes that were already `retryable`, except `policy_unavailable`, which is the platform's own and is thrown before any provider is asked. A provider that breaks its contract (usage that is not a count, or more output than was allowed, which ADR 0039 counts as `provider_unavailable`) is in this group, and is billed for what it reported, so the cost is kept (decision 7). **An answer that was given and is no good is not failed over**: a refusal, an answer that was cut short, text that is not JSON, the wrong shape, and a request that the provider rejected. The first four were paid for, and asking a second provider to do the same job is a second bill for something the caller should decide about (ADR 0039, decision 5); a rejected request is a fault of the request and every provider will say the same.
3. **There is a limit to the attempts in one call**: `maxAttempts`, three unless set, at most ten. The time limit is **for each attempt**, so a call that fails over can take as long as the attempts together, and a caller that needs a bound on the whole gives a signal. The caller's signal is looked at before each attempt, and nothing is sent for a caller who has gone.
4. **Each provider has a circuit breaker.** It counts failures **in a row that are the provider's** (decision 2); a success resets the count. After `failureThreshold` of them (five unless set, at most a hundred) it **opens**, and the provider is not asked for `cooldownMs` (thirty seconds unless set, at most an hour). After that it lets **one** call through (**half open**): if that works it closes, and if it fails it opens again for a whole new cooldown. Calls that arrive while the probe is out go to the other providers, and not to the one that is being tried. A failure that arrives from a call that began before the breaker opened does not make the cooldown longer.
5. **Only what says something about the provider counts for or against it.** An answer that is no good, or a rejection, shows that the provider **was reached**, so it counts as a success for its health, which closes a half-open breaker. A caller who leaves says nothing about the provider, so it counts neither way, and **a probe that was out for such a call is freed** and the next call takes it, so that a caller that leaves cannot keep a provider shut out.
6. **When no provider is in use the call fails at once with `provider_unavailable`, with no attempt, and says that it is retryable.** That is the honest answer, and the caller's own retry, or its budget, decides what is next. When every attempt fails, the code is that of the last.
7. **What every attempt cost is kept.** `ModelError` and `ModelResult` carry `attempts`: for each, the provider, the model, the outcome code and, if the provider answered, the usage (tokens and cost). The cost of a call is **the sum over its attempts**, so a call that was billed for an answer that was no good and then succeeded elsewhere reports both. An attempt that was given up on (time or caller) is not accounted, because its usage is not known, as before. The old single `usage` of an error is replaced by this, and `costMicros` is a property of the error.
8. **The state is in memory, per gateway, per process.** The breaker is not shared between processes, and a restart forgets it. That is enough for this step (a process that finds a provider down finds it again within the threshold) and is not a claim about a fleet. A shared state would need a store and would be a decision of its own.
9. **`gateway.providers()` says what each provider's breaker is** (name, model, state, failures in a row), so that a health check or a log can show it. It has no data of any customer.
10. **The log** has a line at warn level for each attempt that failed (provider, model, code, the number of the attempt, the time), and the line for the call has the number of attempts and the totals. It has no prompt, answer or provider message, as before.

## Consequences

- A provider that is down costs one time limit for each of the first `failureThreshold` calls and nothing after, until the cooldown is over, and then one call. Failover is tested with failures injected through the mock provider: each of the three failures, a provider that is too slow, a crash, a provider with no room, all providers failing, the limit of attempts, the breaker opening, the one probe (including a second call that comes while it is out), its recovery and its failing again, a caller who leaves in the middle (and the probe being freed), and the cost of attempts that were billed and did not answer. 45 faults were planted in the breaker and the gateway one at a time and each is caught by a test (five needed a test made stronger).
- **Not shown:** that any of it holds against a real provider's failures, which look different from a mock's; behaviour with several processes; the effect on latency in practice. **Release gate 16 is in progress, not closed**: it needs the same tests with a real adapter, and no claim of production readiness is made.
- Callers get `attempts` to decide on, and budgets (part three) will use it: the money that was spent is the sum, and a call that was given up on has to be reserved for.

## Planned (not built)

- **Budgets** and kept usage (gate 17); a ledger that survives a restart. **An adapter for a real provider** (needs the owner's decisions). A **shared breaker state**, if the platform runs several processes that call models.
