# 0042. A budget ledger in PostgreSQL

- **Status:** Accepted for the part that is built (a ledger that survives a restart and is shared between processes, and usage kept for each organisation). Who sets the limits, a way to read usage through the API, and an adapter for a real provider are planned and not decided here; see "Planned". Part four of step 8, on top of [ADR 0041](0041-budgets-in-the-model-gateway.md).
- **Date:** 2026-11-26

## Context

[ADR 0041](0041-budgets-in-the-model-gateway.md) made the gateway reserve room before each attempt and settle what was charged, against a ledger it is given. The only ledger was in memory, so it forgot everything when the process ended and was not shared: a deployment with several processes would have allowed the limit to each of them, and a restart would have given the money back. [Release gate 17](../../operations/release-gates.md) asks that budgets are enforced, and [open question 10](../open-questions.md) asks for metering from the start. Both need a ledger that is the same for every process and is still there tomorrow.

## Decisions

1. **The ledger is two tables, in migration 0010, in the database that already holds everything else of a tenant.** `model_budgets` has one row for an organisation: its limit for a calendar month and, optionally, a limit for each task, in whole millionths of a US dollar. `model_ledger_entries` has one row for each thing that happened to the money. Both are tenant tables with row-level security (ADR 0006); the schema guard knows them.
2. **The ledger is append-only.** An entry is one of `reserve` (room taken, at the worst case), `settle` (the difference between what was charged and what was reserved, which can be negative) or `release` (minus what was reserved). What is spent is the sum of the entries, so no row is ever changed or deleted, the application role has `SELECT` and `INSERT` on the table and nothing more, and the history of how the money was spent is the table itself. Deleting entries for retention needs the path that release gate 15 asks for, as for the other append-only tables.
3. **A reservation is closed once.** A unique index on `(organisation_id, reservation_id)` for the entries that are not `reserve` makes a second settle or release insert nothing (`ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`), whichever process makes it and however many at the same time. The closing entry takes the reserved amount, the month and the task **from the reservation's own entry** and not from what the caller remembers of them; a reservation that is not the organisation's is not seen under row-level security, so closing it does nothing. **A reservation that is never closed stays counted as spent, at its worst case**, as ADR 0041 requires: a process that dies between the two cannot make money reappear.
4. **A claim is checked and added under a lock for the organisation.** `reserve` takes a transaction-level advisory lock on a key made from the organisation's id (it needs no privilege on any table and is held to the end of the transaction), reads the limits, sums what the organisation has spent in the month and what the task has spent, and inserts the `reserve` entry only if both fit. Two claims for one organisation are done one after the other, from one process or from two, and so cannot both be granted when they do not both fit. Different organisations do not wait for each other. Closing a reservation is one statement and takes no lock: a claim that does not see it yet is no worse off than one that ran just before it, and a charge above the reservation may pass a limit in any case (ADR 0041, decision 6). The key is made from the lower-case spelling of the id, because PostgreSQL reads a UUID in capitals and in lower case as one organisation and two spellings must not be two locks (a review finding on the first version, which hashed the text as given).
5. **The limits can be read by the application and are written by an operator.** `model_budgets` has no `INSERT`, `UPDATE` or `DELETE` for `codemugs_app`, for the reason that applies to `github_installations` (ADR 0031): a limit that a tenant could raise would not be a limit. It is set with the migration login, and a change takes effect at the next claim, including a limit lowered below what has been spent (every claim is then refused). It is in the schema guard's list of tables that the application only reads. An organisation with **no row is refused**, as in ADR 0041.
6. **The month is the database's.** The period is worked out in the database, in UTC, so that processes with clocks that disagree still agree on which month it is. A reservation carries its month to its closing entry, so a call that starts on the last day of a month and ends in the next is charged to the first. It is read from the clock after the lock is taken (`clock_timestamp()`), not from `now()`, which is the start of the transaction and could be before a month ended that the claim waited through (a review finding). **That is not tested**: the boundary of a month cannot be reached from a test, so a change back to `now()` would not be caught.
7. **What an attempt was is kept, and nothing that was said.** The gateway gives the ledger a record when it settles: the provider's name, the model's name, the outcome as a code, and the input and output tokens where the provider said. Database constraints hold the shapes (names of 1 to 64 and 1 to 200 characters, an outcome of lower-case letters and underscores of at most 40, counts that are not negative) and allow these columns on a `settle` entry only. **No prompt, answer, label, provider message or error text is ever in the table**, and the test of the gateway with the ledger checks it. This is the usage for each organisation that metering needs; reading it through the API is planned. A call that reported usage is recorded **whatever it cost**, a price of nothing included: such a call is settled for nothing with its record. Only an attempt that reported no usage and was charged nothing (a provider that could not be reached, a request it turned away) is released with nothing recorded (a review finding: the first version released every zero-cost attempt).
8. **The ledger fails closed.** A claim that is not a whole amount, a task id that is not valid, an organisation id that is not an id, a database that cannot be reached, a limit or sum that is out of range: each throws, and the gateway turns a throw into `budget_unavailable` with nothing sent and nothing of the message in the error or the log (ADR 0041, decision 4). A charge that is not a whole amount is refused and leaves the reservation open, which keeps it counted. A sum that comes out **negative**, for the month or for a task, throws as well, so it is never taken as credit. Only an operator, or a fault, can make one, but the schema allows a standalone negative `settle` and the application role can insert one for its own organisation; a trigger that makes every `settle` and `release` answer to a `reserve` would close that, and is not built (a review finding on the first version, which compared such a sum with the limit).
9. **The gateway checks the length of a model's name** (200 characters, as the table does), so that a record that the table would refuse is a configuration fault found when the gateway is made and not a failure to settle in the middle of a call.
10. **The ledger is a package of its own, `@codemugs/model-budget`**, which depends on the gateway (for the interface) and on storage (for tenant transactions). The gateway stays free of the database, as it was free of everything else, and a deployment that wants another ledger gives another.

## Planned (not built)

- **Who sets the limits**, and what the defaults are, are decisions for the owner (a monthly spend cap is one of the things the real provider's adapter waits for, [open question 10](../open-questions.md)). Nothing sets one, so every organisation is refused until an operator inserts a row.
- **A tool for an operator to set and show limits**, and an audit entry when one changes.
- **Usage read through the API**, for an organisation to see what it has spent, and alerts at a share of a limit.
- **Retention** of old entries (release gate 15). Entries are kept until then; a sum over a month is one indexed read, and the sum for a task reads all its entries, which is fine while a task has a few and is to be looked at again if one has many.
- **A sweep for reservations that were never closed** (the process died). They count as spent, at the worst case, which is safe and is also a loss of room; a sweep that releases the stale ones would need to know that the call is over, which a lease would say. Not decided here.
- **An adapter for a real provider**; limits on the rate of calls, which are a different thing from money.

## Consequences

- The limit is one limit for every process that shares the database, and it is still there after a restart. In tests, eighty claims of ten against room for five hundred, made at once from two pools (two processes), grant exactly fifty; two gateways on two pools stop together when the money is gone; a claim is refused at the limit and not before, by one unit; a limit changed by an operator is taken at the next claim; an organisation, a month and a task are each kept apart; a reservation is closed once however it is closed; the application role cannot change a limit or change or delete an entry; and a tenant sees its own entries and limits and no other's.
- After review, six more faults were planted in the fixes (the spelling of the lock key, a negative sum accepted for the month, for a task and at the edge of -1) and four in the gateway's choice between release and settle: all ten are caught.
- 42 faults were planted in the ledger and the gateway's side of it, one at a time (limits one unit either way or ignored, a claim not added to what is spent, a month or a task counted wrongly, a refusal named wrongly, a close that writes the charge and not the difference or loses the month, the task or the organisation of the reservation, a second close that is not turned away, an invalid claim or charge taken, the usage record dropped, swapped or wrong, the lock left out of a claim, the length of a model's name). Seven survived the first round: two needed a test added (the month that a close carries, and a sum that cannot be right), and **five survive and are equivalent**: a lock key shared by all organisations (it only makes them wait for each other), a task id that is not checked in the package (the table's constraint refuses it all the same), `ON CONFLICT` without its target (the one index is the only conflict possible), a sum for a task not restricted to the organisation (row-level security does it), and a read-only transaction made writable (nothing in it writes). These are defence in depth, and are kept.
- Every claim and every closing is a transaction with a lock and a few statements, each a round trip to the database, where a call in memory was none; it is in front of a model call that takes seconds. A busy organisation does its claims one at a time; that is the price of a limit that holds, and it can be looked at again if it shows up in measurements, which have not been made.
- A reservation that is made and not closed because the process died takes its worst case out of the month. Until a sweep exists the limit is, in that case, a little lower than the money that was spent.
- **Not shown:** the ledger under a real provider's billing; a database that fails in the middle of a settle in a deployment (the transaction is the unit, and a failure leaves the reservation counted, which is tested only as far as a pool that cannot connect); that an operator's limit is what the owner meant, because there is no tool and no owner decision yet. **Release gate 17 is in progress, not closed**: nothing sets a limit, no real provider is behind the gateway, and nothing calls it.
