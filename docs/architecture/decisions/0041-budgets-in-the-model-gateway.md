# 0041. Budgets in the model gateway

- **Status:** Accepted for the part that is built (enforcement, with a ledger in memory). A ledger that survives a restart, and usage kept per organisation, are planned and not decided here; see "Planned". Part three of step 8, on top of [ADR 0039](0039-the-model-gateway.md) and [ADR 0040](0040-failover-and-a-circuit-breaker.md).
- **Date:** 2026-11-25

## Context

[Release gate 17](../../operations/release-gates.md) asks that cost limits and execution budgets are enforced, shown by tests that budgets halt work. A model call costs money that is not known until it is over, and work that loops, or a customer that sends a great deal, can spend without end. [Open question 10](../open-questions.md) asks for model quotas and metering from the start. The gateway is the one place every model call passes through, so it is where the money has to stop.

## Decisions

1. **A ledger is an interface that the gateway is given.** `reserve(claim)` takes room for one attempt (the organisation, the task if there is one, and an amount) and answers with a reservation, or says which limit is in the way (the organisation's or the task's). A reservation is **settled** with what the attempt was charged, or **released** if nothing was spent; either happens once and a second call does nothing. `reserve` must be atomic: two claims that do not both fit are not both granted. **A reservation that is never settled stays counted as spent, at its worst case**, so that a process that dies between the two cannot make money reappear.
2. **There is no default.** `budget` is a required option, as `policy` is, because a default that spends without limit is not one to fall back on. `unlimitedBudget()` is for tests and development and says so in its name.
3. **Room is reserved before each attempt at a provider, for the worst that the attempt can cost**: the input as guessed (the guess of ADR 0039, which errs towards too big) and all of the output that the request allows, at that provider's price. A call that fails over reserves again for each provider. Nothing is sent without a reservation, and a reservation that does not fit ends the call.
4. **A refusal ends the call and is not failed over.** No room is `budget_exceeded` (not retryable: another try will not help until the budget changes) and a ledger that cannot be read is `budget_unavailable` (retryable, and it fails closed: nothing is sent). A call that has made attempts carries them in the error. Choosing a cheaper provider when the first does not fit is routing by cost, and is not decided here. A refusal says nothing about the provider, so it does not count against its breaker, and a probe that it held is freed (ADR 0040, decision 5).
5. **What an attempt is charged** is the cost where the provider said what it used (an answer that could not be used was billed all the same); the **worst case where the provider may have billed and did not say** (it was too slow, the caller left, it gave usage that is not a count or too large to be a cost, it reported its own timeout, or an adapter failed with an error of its own); and **nothing where it cannot have** (it could not be reached, it limited the requests, it rejected the request). Under-counting is how a cap is broken, so doubt is charged. The attempt records this as `chargedMicros`, and the error and the result carry the sum next to `costMicros`, which stays what the providers said. This closes the gap that ADR 0039 left for a call that is given up on.
6. **The charge is what happened.** It can be more than was reserved when the guess of the input was too low, and the ledger records it as it is; the next claim sees it and is refused. Calls made at once cannot together pass a limit by more than that, because each has reserved its worst case before it is sent.
7. **Two scopes.** The organisation has a limit for each calendar month (UTC), counted from the month in which a reservation was made. A task has a limit of its own, by an opaque id in the call's context (`[A-Za-z0-9_-]`, 1 to 64 characters; it is given by platform code, like the organisation, and never read from a request). The organisation's limit always applies; the task's applies only to calls that name a task. An organisation whose limits nobody set is refused. The limits belong to the ledger, not to the gateway or the caller.
8. **A failure to settle does not fail the call.** The money has been spent. The reservation stays (which counts it as spent, at its worst case) and the log has a warning with the organisation, the task and the amount, and nothing that the ledger said. A reservation that is granted after the caller has left, or after the wait for the ledger was given up, is given back, and so is one that is granted just as the caller leaves (the signal is looked at again after the grant, because an attempt looks at it only after it has called the provider).
9. **A schema that throws on an answer is an answer that does not fit.** It is `output_invalid`, with the usage and the charge, as for any answer that was billed and is no good. Before this it escaped as `provider_unavailable` with no record of the attempt.
10. **The ledger in the package is in memory**, per process: it forgets everything when the process ends, it is not shared, and it keeps an entry for every task it has seen. It is for tests and development.

## Planned (not built)

- **A ledger that survives a restart and is shared between processes**: tables with tenant isolation, an atomic reserve in one statement, a sweep that treats a stale reservation as spent, and **usage kept per organisation** for metering (a migration, and the next part of step 8).
- **Who sets the limits**, and what the defaults are, are decisions for the owner (a monthly spend cap is one of the things the real provider's adapter waits for, [open question 10](../open-questions.md)). Nothing here sets one.
- **An adapter for a real provider**; limits on the rate of calls, which are a different thing from money.

## Consequences

- Work stops when the money does, in tests: an organisation at its limit for the month, a call whose worst case does not fit though its cost would, an organisation with a limit of nothing or none, a task at its limit while another task and a call with no task go on, ten calls at once against room for four (four are sent), a failed-over call whose first attempt was charged the worst case leaving no room for the second, a ledger that cannot be read (nothing is sent, and nothing of its message is in the error or the log), a reservation granted after the caller left, a ledger that fails to settle. The charge of each kind of failure is tested, and the ledger's own rules (limits to the unit, periods, scopes, atomicity, a second settle doing nothing).
- 66 faults were planted in the ledger and the gateway one at a time and each is caught by a test (eight needed a test added or made stronger, three of them after a review found that a caller who leaves in the moment after the grant could still have had the call sent).
- **Not shown:** a ledger that survives a restart or is shared, so **a deployment with several processes, each with a ledger in memory, allows the limit to each of them**; that the guess of the input is close to what a real provider counts; how a real provider bills a call that was given up on. **Release gate 17 is in progress, not closed**, and no claim is made that spending is capped.
